# Preliminary venue fit (not a submission recommendation)

**Scope:** based on the current software/workflow claim and repo evidence as of 2026-09-30. Venue scope and policies can change; recheck official author instructions before submission. No acceptance probability is implied.

| Venue / category | Why the subject may fit | Likely novelty / validation expectation | Main current gap |
|---|---|---|---|
| Journal of Open Source Software (JOSS) — research software | Open-source computational research tool with tests, CLI/API and reproducibility focus. JOSS requires public, licensed software, documentation/tests and evidence of actual research use; it also currently expects sustained public development history and iterative use. [Official submission criteria](https://joss.readthedocs.io/en/latest/submitting.html) | Software quality, installability, documentation, tests, research use/impact and open practices; not a substitute for a biological methods paper. | Confirm OSI license, public repo history/age, tagged release, citation-ready package, user-facing docs, CI and evidence the software is used in research. Need inspect current license/public history before deciding readiness. |
| Frontiers in Neuroinformatics — neuroscience software/methods | The project orchestrates neuroscience models/data and formalizes computational experiments; venue scope is directly neuroinformatics. [Journal](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/neuroinformatics) | Clear neuroscience use case, technical contribution, reproducibility, and a convincing account of utility to neuroscience researchers. | External scientific review, second-operator reproduction, complete end-to-end demonstration, direct comparison to FlyBrainLab/Mozaik, and evidence beyond a score workflow. |
| PLOS Computational Biology — Methods or Software | The topic intersects computational neuroscience and software. Official criteria call for outstanding methods/software with potential for broad adoption and biological insight; rigorous methodology and substantial evidence are expected. [Journal criteria](https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/s/journal-information) | Strong originality/importance and methodological or biological insight; reproducible data/code; software paper should offer substantial capabilities rather than a thin integration. | Present evidence establishes utility as a workflow only; gate benefit is unidentifiable in the benchmark and full Workbench does not beat effect-only. Need broad user value and stronger real-world evaluation. |
| Journal of Computational Neuroscience — computational methods/modeling | Potential fit if the manuscript centers on formal computational experiment design around a neural model and evaluates it in neuroscience practice. [Journal](https://link.springer.com/journal/10827) | A substantive computational-neuroscience contribution, not only packaging; model assumptions and interpretation must be rigorous. | No new neural method/model, no biological validation, no predictive behavior claim. Fit weakens if the paper remains mostly platform plumbing. |
| SoftwareX — research software article | Possible software-first venue for describing reusable scientific software and its application. [Journal](https://www.sciencedirect.com/journal/softwarex) | Clear software functionality, availability, reusable use case, and documented/reproducible example. Check current article template and criteria. | Release engineering, license, stable installation, external reproduction and demonstrated use case; careful distinction from established fly platforms. |
| Bioinformatics — software/methods route (conditional) | Could fit only if the contribution becomes a broadly useful computational method or reusable resource beyond a single Drosophila case. [Journal](https://academic.oup.com/bioinformatics) | Methodological novelty and broad computational-biology relevance; fit depends on current article type and author guidance. | Current narrow model-specific benchmark and workflow integration may be insufficiently general; no method superiority or broad uptake established. |

## Practical interpretation

The current record most naturally supports a **software/methods framing**, not a biological-discovery paper. JOSS/SoftwareX focus more on software quality and use; Frontiers in Neuroinformatics is the clearest domain-oriented software/methods fit; PLOS Computational Biology or Bioinformatics would need stronger methodological distinction and evidence. Revisit after the second-operator run, external case review, held-out gate decision, and direct prior-system comparison. Do not treat held-out execution as a novelty remedy: even a positive retrospective benchmark does not establish biological utility.
